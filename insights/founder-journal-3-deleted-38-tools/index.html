Founder Journal #3: The Week I Deleted 38 Tools — and Stopped Chasing

ToolPixi went from 160 tools to 139 by deleting what nobody searches for. MythPost went from 146 articles to 361. The numbers are still small — and one of them is lying to me.

Share this insight Share on X Facebook WhatsApp LinkedIn

A week ago I wrote that a journal which hides its own errors is just marketing. The rule has not changed for this third one: every number below is verified, and anything I could not verify is not in it. This was the week I stopped adding and started subtracting — and the week I learned that one of my own dashboards has been flattering me.

ToolPixi is the biggest story. On November 23 I audited all 160 of its tools against real worldwide search demand over the last one to two years — not our own Search Console numbers, which only show what people already found, but what people around the world actually search for and use. Thirty-eight tools failed that test: sixteen single-holiday countdown pages, five near-identical "to WebP" converters, thirteen small text and developer tools nobody looks for, and a couple of borderline ones. I removed all 38, with redirects to the nearest live tool, and the site went from 160 tools to 122, its sitemap from 195 pages to 157. Then I built back only what the demand proved: ten high-demand tools, including a Typing Speed Test, a Meme Generator, a QR Code Scanner and a Resume Builder, followed by seven professional PDF tools — Crop, Unlock, Protect, Repair, Organize, Compare, and PDF to Excel. The site now stands at 139 tools with a 174-page sitemap. The PDF to Excel tool had to meet a bar I set myself: it had to work the way iLovePDF's does, with no practical limitation. It did — cell-perfect on all three test files, including a scanned one handled by OCR. The lesson is simple and I should have learned it sooner: piling on tools nobody searches for does not build traffic. It builds a catalogue nobody reads.

The same week, ToolPixi's design went through three rejections. A first redesign went live and I rejected it. A second version went live and I rejected that too — it did not look like the reference I had picked. So I changed the process instead of arguing about the result: study the reference properly first, build mockup pictures, send them to me, and deploy only after I approve. I even sent screenshots from my own phone so the mobile pattern was measured, not guessed. The fifth version was approved with two conditions — it must not look like a copy of the reference, and even its structure must feel different — and it is live now, in ToolPixi's own deep-jewel colours with its own layout. Two rejected deploys taught me more than two approved ones would have: the process was the problem, not the designer.

MythPost kept growing fast. It went from 146 articles when I wrote Journal #2 to 361 today. Its Search Console figures, for November 10 to 19, are 4,581 impressions, 36 clicks, a 0.8% click-through rate, and an average position of 10.2 — the closest any of my sites has come to page one. But Google Discover is still at zero: no clicks, no impressions, no Discover report at all. No MythPost article has ever appeared there. The net-worth cluster I started as the evergreen engine is working the way I hoped: the Jensen Huang page was deepened in place from 1,246 words to 3,127, and it is now the site's most-clicked page. And the small-keyword lane is live beside the hot lane, with four tests on every story: real demand, no big site in the top ten, a long specific question, and striking distance first.

Now the number that is lying to me. My own dashboard says MythPost had 16,004 visitors and 19,369 page views in the last seven days. Google Analytics says the site had about 74 sessions in the last 28 days. Both cannot be describing the same readers, and they are not. The Cloudflare count includes bots, crawlers and automated traffic; the Analytics count is closer to real people. I am writing both numbers down because the gap matters: if I believed the bigger one, I would think the monetisation problem is solved. It is not. Traffic, not the ad network, is still the gate. That is also why AdSense stays parked, Monetag is gone from all four sites, and Media.net — applied for on November 21 — has still not replied. The follow-up goes out on Friday, November 27, and only if there is still no reply.

The other two sites moved quietly. Grab AI Tool now lists 608 tools, with 81 alternatives guides and 23 comparison pages, and its Search Console figures for October 22 to November 18 are 7,442 impressions, 9 clicks, and an average position of 43.3. DailyWallPix holds 5,133 wallpapers, and its last 28 days show 9 clicks, 607 impressions, and an average position of 21.9, with its Christmas aesthetic page at position 13.6. CEO Legacy, my Facebook page, is unchanged at 22,000 followers. None of these numbers is exciting. All of them are honest, and honest numbers are the only ones worth planning on.

Some of the week's best work was invisible. The "Built by Hamza Ali Basra" credit came off the footers of all four sites, so the footers are brand-only now, with the founder's presence kept to a short note on the About pages. The Search Console indexing queue stands at 847 URLs; ten went through on the night of November 23 — ToolPixi's Barcode Generator, the MythPost horoscope hub, and eight zodiac sign pages — and four more are queued first for the next run because the daily quota ran out. A deployment blocker was found and fixed for good: Cloudflare caps deployments at 20,000 files and DailyWallPix's full site is over 37,000, so it now deploys from a staging folder of about 5,250 files. And a free-listing offer for Grab arrived by email and I declined it in one line. Not every opportunity is worth the reply.

Now the failures, because there were several. My Search Console session died on November 22 — all three Google accounts signed out at once — and I signed back in myself that night; the rule stands that only the daily indexing run touches Search Console. The Bold Journey coverage I have been waiting on is still unverified; even I could not get past its human-verification check in the browser. The Rite Aid documentary, the last footage video for Empire Archive TV, is parked on my own instruction — I told my assistant to leave it alone. The cold outreach I started weeks ago has now produced 52 approaches and zero replies, so the follow-ups are stopped; chasing harder was not the answer. And the closest call of the week: I believed I had already cancelled a WeTransfer trial, but a live check showed it was still active and set to start charging $9.99 a week from November 26. It is cancelled now, verified in the browser, with an email confirmation. Never take my own word that a money step is done — check it live.

What happens next is mostly waiting, done properly. Google's core update, which started on November 10, ends around today; I will check Search Console a week after it ends and fix only the pages that actually lost clicks. The Media.net follow-up goes out Friday if there is still no reply. The work itself does not change: striking-distance pages first, depth on the pages people already find, and no new tool, article or page without a demand check first. The week taught me that deleting can be building, that a process fix beats a second argument, and that the most dangerous number in my business is the one that flatters me. I would rather have 74 real sessions and know it than 16,000 visitors and believe it.

If you are building something of your own, the four ventures behind this journal — Grab AI Tool, ToolPixi, DailyWallPix and MythPost — are where I test everything I write here, and the contact page is open if you want to talk about any of it.
