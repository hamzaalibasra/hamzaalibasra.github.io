Make the Offer Obvious

If a stranger cannot explain what you sell in one sentence, you do not have a marketing problem. You have a clarity problem.

Share this insight Share on X Facebook WhatsApp LinkedIn

I have watched good products lose to weaker ones, and the reason was rarely quality. The customer could not figure out, in the first ten seconds, what the thing was, who it was for, and what to do next. Confusion does not argue with you. It just leaves, quietly, and buys from the person who made the decision easy.

We love to add. Another feature, another package, another clever name that only makes sense inside our own heads. Clever feels smart when you are building. It feels like work when you are buying. Nobody wakes up wanting to decode an offer. People want to recognize themselves in it: this is my problem, this is the result, this is the next step.

So I run a simple test on my own work. Explain the offer to a stranger in one sentence, without a follow-up sentence to rescue it. If that sentence needs a 'well, actually,' it is not an offer yet. It is a puzzle. One person, one problem, one clear next step — that is the whole test, and most businesses fail it before they ever get to price.

Clarity is also a form of respect. Say what is included, say what is not, and say what happens after someone says yes. Hidden steps, vague promises, and surprise conditions do not make an offer look bigger. They make it look riskier. Trust grows when the customer can see the whole path before they take the first step.

None of this means dumbing your work down. The work can be deep, detailed, and expert. The doorway into it should not be. Let the cleverness live inside the product, where it earns its keep. Keep the front door plain, well-lit, and easy to walk through.

Make the offer obvious, and you will hear the difference in how people talk about you. They will describe it correctly to someone else, without you in the room. That is the real test of a business: not whether you can explain it, but whether your customers can. When they can, they do your selling for you.
