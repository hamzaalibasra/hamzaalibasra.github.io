Founder Journal #1: A New Site in One Day — and What the Numbers Actually Say

Yesterday I launched a fourth website in a single day. Here are the real numbers behind it — the wins, the blocks, and what I am doing next.

Share this insight Share on X Facebook WhatsApp LinkedIn

This is the first entry in a new kind of post on this site. No more generic essays. From now on, twice a week, I am writing down what is actually happening in my work: the real numbers, what shipped, what failed, and what I learned. If a number is in this journal, I verified it. If I could not verify it, it is not here.

The headline of this week is simple: on November 10, I launched a fourth website, MythPost, in a single day. Forty-two complete articles, fifty-seven pages in total, one hundred sixty-one files deployed to Cloudflare Pages, and 3,654 internal links checked with none broken. The site went live the same evening at mythpost.pages.dev. The domain, mythpost.com, is bought and the nameservers were changed the same day, so the custom domain is now waiting on activation.

One day sounds fast, and it was — but only because the slow work was already done. The publishing system, the article templates, the image rules, the policy pages, the deployment route through Cloudflare: all of that existed before November 10. The launch was fast because the machine was ready. That is the honest version of the story. Speed is almost never a miracle. It is preparation meeting a deadline.

Here is where the four sites actually stand, as of November 10. Grab AI Tool, my AI-tools directory, now lists 567 tools. In Google Search Console, over the 28 days to November 7, it earned 14 clicks and 2,020 impressions across 1,005 queries, at an average position of 31.7. Its strongest page is the Kling 4.0 page: 253 impressions at position 12.5 — and zero clicks. That gap between impressions and clicks is the work in front of me. ToolPixi, my free-tools site, now has 127 tools, with 20 clicks and 2,433 impressions at position 38.9 in the same period. DailyWallPix, the wallpaper site, holds 3,351 wallpapers, with 2 clicks and 75 impressions at position 14.2. Small numbers. Moving in the right direction, but small — and I would rather print small honest numbers than large invented ones.

Two more numbers from the same week. On November 10 I requested indexing in Search Console for four Grab AI Tool category pages — AI audio tools, AI business tools, AI code tools, and AI design tools — and all four went through. And CEO Legacy, my Facebook page, stands at 22,000 followers. That page is the clearest proof I have that consistency compounds: it was built one post at a time, every day, with no shortcuts.

Now the part most founders leave out: what failed this week. On Pinterest, zero of ten DailyWallPix pins went live because the domain is blocked, and the appeal is still pending. On Tumblr, the account signed out again with five posts ready and waiting on a sign-in I still need to do. On Reddit, one warm-up comment was removed by AutoModerator. And on the content side, I built a new distance-comparison reel format twice in one day — and rejected it twice. It is dropped for good. Not every experiment deserves a third try.

The pattern in those failures is worth naming. None of them were content problems. The content shipped. The distribution broke. Pins blocked, an account signed out, a comment filtered, a format that did not land. When you build in public, distribution is usually the bottleneck, not the work itself. So that is where the next effort goes: fixing the Pinterest block, getting Tumblr signed back in, and keeping the publishing cadence steady while the search numbers climb.

What I am doing next is straightforward. First, finish the MythPost domain activation and submit its sitemap to Search Console once mythpost.com is live. Second, keep publishing five to ten original articles a day on MythPost while the trend is fresh, and keep the daily posting cadence going on the Facebook pages. Third, work the click gap on Grab: pages already ranking on page two and three need better titles and better answers, not more pages. The goal for the next journal is simple — report whether those three things moved the numbers, in either direction.

If you are building something of your own, take this from the week: count what is real, write it down, and let the small numbers tell you where to work next. The launch is the easy part to celebrate. The numbers are the part that tells the truth.
