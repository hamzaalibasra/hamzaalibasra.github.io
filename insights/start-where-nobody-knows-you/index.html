Start Where Nobody Knows Your Name

A new city gives you the one thing your hometown never could: a clean record. Nobody knows your old story — so you get to write the next one.

Share this insight Share on X Facebook WhatsApp LinkedIn

There is a strange freedom in building somewhere nobody knows your name. No old story follows you into the room. Nobody remembers the version of you that played it safe, or the project that did not work, or the year you spent figuring things out. In a new city, you are not your history. You are your next move.

Los Angeles taught me this fast. It is a city full of people starting over — actors between roles, founders between ventures, dreamers between plans. Nobody asks where you came from for long. They ask what you are building now. That question, asked often enough, becomes a kind of fuel. You stop explaining your past and start defending your present.

Most people treat being unknown as a disadvantage. They want the warm introduction, the familiar room, the network they already have. But a network you already have is also a ceiling you already know. Strangers owe you nothing — and that is the point. Every relationship you build from zero is built on the work itself, not on who you used to be.

Starting fresh also forces honesty. You cannot coast on a reputation nobody has heard of. Your product has to speak. Your consistency has to speak. Your follow-through has to speak. That pressure is uncomfortable, and it is the best teacher I have ever had. Comfort never taught me anything worth keeping.

So if you ever get the chance to start somewhere new, take it. Or build like you did. Act as if nobody knows your name yet, because in the market that matters, they mostly don't. Introduce yourself with the work, not the story. Let the city learn your name the same way every great brand earned its own: one kept promise at a time.

A clean slate is not a setback. It is the rarest advantage in business — the chance to be judged only by what you do next.
