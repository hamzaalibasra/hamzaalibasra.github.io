Karma Is Just Reputation With a Long Memory

You can call it karma or call it the market remembering. Either way, how you treat people when you don't need them decides what happens when you do.

Share this insight Share on X Facebook WhatsApp LinkedIn

People talk about karma like it is weather — something mysterious that falls on the lucky and the unlucky. In business, I have learned it is much simpler than that. Karma is reputation, and reputation has a very long memory.

Every market is smaller than it looks. The agent you cheated in one deal drinks coffee with the investor you need in the next one. The client you ignored after taking their money tells their story at every dinner for a decade. Nothing travels faster than how you treated someone when you thought it didn't matter.

The reverse compounds just as quietly. The deal you did honestly at a loss. The call you returned when there was nothing in it for you. The young person you guided without charging. Those deposits sit in accounts you cannot see — until the day an opportunity arrives with your name already attached to it.

This is why I never chase short-term wins at the price of my name. Money lost can be earned again in a season. A name lost in a small market can take a lifetime, and sometimes it never comes back.

So treat every interaction like it will be repeated in rooms you will never enter — because it will be. Play the long game with people, and the long game has a way of finding you. Call it karma. I call it the oldest business law there is.

None of this requires being soft. You can negotiate hard, walk away from bad deals, and fire the wrong people — and still have every one of them respect how you did it. Strength and decency are not opposites. In the long run, they are the same strategy.
