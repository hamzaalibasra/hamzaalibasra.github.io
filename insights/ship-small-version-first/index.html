Ship the Small Version First

Perfection is not a launch strategy. Ship the small honest version, let real people use it, and let their feedback build version two.

Share this insight Share on X Facebook WhatsApp LinkedIn

Most founders do not fail because their idea was bad. They fail because their idea never left the notebook. They keep adding features and waiting for a version so polished that nobody could criticize it. That version never arrives, because perfection is not a finish line. It is a hiding place, and every month spent hiding is a month the market spends learning without you.

Ship the small version first. Not a broken version, and not a promise dressed up as a product — one honest thing that works, solves one real problem, and can be put in front of real people this month. If it helps ten strangers, you have something. If it helps nobody, you have learned something planning could never teach you. Either way, you are finally moving on evidence instead of opinion.

Real users are the best editors you will ever have. They will ignore the feature you spent weeks perfecting and ask for the one you thought was too simple to matter. Their questions will tell you exactly what version two should be. You cannot get that feedback from a planning document. You can only get it from something real, in someone else's hands.

This is also a test of character. A small launch forces you to be honest about what you actually built. It is uncomfortable, and that is the point. Founders who can stand behind a small, honest version can be trusted with a bigger one. Founders who only talk about the grand version usually stay exactly where they are — talking.

I meet builders every week who are one decision away from starting: the decision to stop preparing and start shipping. Pick the smallest version that still deserves your name on it. Give it a deadline measured in weeks, not seasons. Then release it, listen carefully, fix what matters, and ship again. That rhythm — small, honest, repeated — is how a venture actually grows.

Do not wait until you feel ready. You will not feel ready, and readiness was never the requirement. Usefulness was. Ship the small version first, and let the work grow up in front of the people it serves. The builders who win are rarely the ones with the grandest plan. They are the ones who put something real in the world first.
