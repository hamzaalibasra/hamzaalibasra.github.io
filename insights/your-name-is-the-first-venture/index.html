Your Name Is the First Venture

Before anyone trusts your product, they decide whether they trust the person behind it. Build your name like a venture.

Share this insight Share on X Facebook WhatsApp LinkedIn

Every founder I know is building two things. There is the company they can describe in a pitch, and there is the person whose name is quietly attached to it. The second one is the first venture. And most founders never build it on purpose.

Think about it. Before anyone trusts your product, they decide whether they trust the person behind it. They type your name into a search bar before they ever type your brand. What they find there — a clear record of how you think, what you have built, what you stand for — is either working for you or it is empty, and empty is also an answer.

The part most people get wrong is that they treat their name like decoration. They polish the logo, tune the product, optimize the funnel, and leave their own presence as an afterthought — a profile picture from five years ago and a bio that says nothing. Then they wonder why strangers feel no confidence in them. Confidence does not start at the checkout page. It starts at the first impression of the founder.

Your name is an asset that compounds the same way money does. Every honest thing you publish under it. Every person you helped without charging. Every project you finished and stood behind. These are deposits. Five years of deposits turn a name into collateral — investors pick up the phone faster, partners assume competence, customers forgive one mistake because the record is long. Nobody can buy that. It can only be built.

So build it like you build a company: deliberately. Own your domain before someone else does. Put your ideas in your own words, publicly, on something you control. Make it so that when someone asks who you are, the internet answers correctly. This is not ego. It is infrastructure. A brand can be sold, renamed, or sunset. Your name follows you to every room you will ever enter.

The product you launch this year may change. The market will change, guaranteed. The one venture you will carry through every pivot, every win, and every failure is the name on the door. Start building it today, the way you would build anything that matters — one honest, visible piece of work at a time. Your company is a venture. Your name is the first one.
