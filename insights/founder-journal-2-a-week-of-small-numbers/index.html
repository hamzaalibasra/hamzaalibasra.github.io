Founder Journal #2: A Week of Small Numbers and Hard Lessons

One week after launch, MythPost has 146 articles and its first AdSense review. The numbers are still small — and they are still telling the truth.

Share this insight Share on X Facebook WhatsApp LinkedIn

A week ago I wrote the first Founder Journal on the day I launched MythPost. This is the second one, and the rule has not changed: every number in it is verified, and anything I could not verify is not in it. The week since has been the busiest of the four sites' short lives, and the honest summary is that a lot shipped, the numbers moved a little, and the failures taught more than the wins.

MythPost is the headline. It went from 42 articles at launch to 146 today. Ten hot stories went up before mid-morning today alone — a bank outage, a Nasdaq correction, a United Airlines outage, a Drake Maye injury scare, LeBron James answering an ESPN report, a FEMA alert-test rumor, and more — each one published within an hour or two of the trend, each with a verdict box and a timeline. Google Search Console started collecting MythPost impressions on November 11, and by November 13 the site was at 107 impressions a day, 219 in total, 7 clicks, at an average position of 12.5. Small, but it is the closest to page one of any of my four sites. In the early hours of this morning I applied for AdSense on MythPost. Ownership verified on the third attempt, and the site is now in review under a new publisher account. The first two attempts failed because I entered the publisher ID with a wrong digit. That was my mistake, not Google's, and I am writing it down here because a journal that hides its own errors is just marketing.

The other three sites kept moving too. Grab AI Tool now lists 582 tools, up from 567 a week ago, and twelve new comparison and alternatives pages went live today as part of a fifteen-day SEO push that started yesterday. On November 13 it earned 390 impressions in a day, with 5,701 impressions and 12 clicks over 28 days at an average position of 41.9. ToolPixi grew from 127 tools to 138, including a new test page that compresses an image to exactly 50KB — the kind of specific page people actually search for — and its November 13 numbers were 228 impressions a day, 3,643 over 28 days, 26 clicks, at position 36. Its Age Difference Calculator sits at position 11.7, the closest any ToolPixi page has come to page one. DailyWallPix now holds 4,147 wallpapers, and its Christmas collection keeps climbing: 6 clicks and 422 impressions over 28 days at an average position of 18.4, with the Christmas aesthetic page at 14.2. CEO Legacy, my Facebook page, is unchanged at 22,000 followers.

The most useful lesson of the week came from a question I asked myself. Search Console showed Grab at 6,460 impressions over three months, but Monetag, the ad network, showed only 13 or 14 impressions. For a moment that looked like something was broken. It was not. The two numbers count different things: Search Console counts a page appearing in search results, Monetag counts a real visitor actually opening the site and loading an ad. At position 41.9, most of those 6,460 appearances never became a visit — only 18 clicks in three months. The problem is not the ad code, which is verified live. The problem is position, and position is the work.

Distribution got real effort this week. The MythPost backlink campaign's second wave closed out: a LinkedIn company page, a Crunchbase profile, and a Bluesky account are all live; Bing Webmaster Tools verified the site; NewsBreak and Ground News applications are submitted and under review; one story is in Fark's submission queue. Two items are stuck on decisions only I can make: SmartNews requires a US tax ID I do not have, and Scoop.it needs a compliant password from me. I will not invent a tax number to get past a form. Some doors stay closed until the honest key exists.

Now the failures, because there were several. My Search Console session died on November 16 — all three Google accounts signed out at once — and I renewed it myself that afternoon; the rule stands that only the daily 5 PM run touches Search Console, and nothing else signs in or out. A US check of Monetag found no ads at all on any of the three sites from an American datacenter connection, even though the ad code is live in the page — the likely cause is the network withholding ads from datacenter IPs, and the real test is the dashboard's statistics by country. Hashnode is dropped for good: its human-verification check failed repeatedly, even when I tried it myself. Pinterest still blocks DailyWallPix link pins, nine days and counting, though image-only pins went through ten out of ten. And on the earning front, 52 cold approaches have now produced zero replies. This morning I told my assistant to stop the follow-ups. Chasing harder was not the answer; the answer is a stronger product and better timing.

I also asked this week why my sites are not in the top three results when the whole world seems to manage it. The honest answer is that position is not a setting or a button. Google gives it. My sites are five to six weeks old, competing against sites that are years old with hundreds of thousands of backlinks, in the middle of a Google core update that runs until November 24. The route is the striking-distance pages — the ones already at position 11 or 14 — pushed first, then authority outward. I checked a site a friend recommended as a traffic success story, OftensMagazine: under 20,000 monthly visits and a global rank around 1.7 million. Its real secret was not a trick. It publishes five or six evergreen long-tail articles a day. Volume on topics nobody fights over. That is a lesson worth copying honestly.

What happens next is already scheduled. On November 23 and November 30 I will re-check Search Console against the November 13 baseline and report whether the fifteen-day SEO push moved anything — Grab 390, ToolPixi 228, DailyWallPix 72, MythPost 107 impressions a day. Until then the work is the same as last week: publish MythPost's hot stories while they are hot, keep the daily Facebook cadence going, and improve the pages that are already close to page one instead of building new ones nobody asked for. The numbers are small. They are also the only honest map I have, and I intend to keep following them.
