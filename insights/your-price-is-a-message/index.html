Your Price Is a Message

Before anyone reads your proposal, they have already read your price. Make sure it says what you mean.

Share this insight Share on X Facebook WhatsApp LinkedIn

Nobody reads your proposal before they read your price. The number sits at the bottom of the page, quiet, doing more talking than every paragraph above it. It tells the client what you think of your own work before you have said a word. I did not learn this from a book. I learned it from years of watching people decide what my work was worth by reading the number I had put on it.

Here is the uncomfortable part: a low price does not buy you more customers. It buys you harder customers. The person who negotiates you down on price will negotiate you down on everything — the timeline, the revisions, the respect. Discount buyers are not loyal. They are rented. They leave the moment someone cheaper shows up, and they were never really yours to begin with.

Every discount teaches the market a lesson you did not mean to teach. Discount once, and people learn to wait for the next one. Explain your low price as a special case, and it becomes your actual price. The number you charge sets the story about the number you are. Raise it with confidence and something strange happens: the serious people take you more seriously, and the people who were never going to value the work filter themselves out. That is not a loss. That is the filter doing its job.

This is not about being expensive for the sake of it. It is about alignment. Charge little and you will be forced to cut corners to survive, and the work quietly becomes what the price said it was — cheap. Charge what the work is honestly worth and you can afford to do it properly: the follow-through, the care, the details nobody claps for but everybody feels.

So audit the message your price sends. If you flinch when you say it, that is not humility — that is the market reading you before you speak. Raise your price to the level your work already lives at, then raise your work until it deserves the next one. Your price is never just a number. It is the loudest sentence in your business.
