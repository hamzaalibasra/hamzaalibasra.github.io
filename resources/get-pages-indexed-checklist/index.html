Get Your New Pages Found on Google — Checklist

The daily indexing routine behind Hamza's four websites: one Search Console door, about ten requests a day, and the discipline of a queue — how new pages actually get found.

Publishing a page and Google finding it are two different jobs. Hamza runs four websites — Grab AI Tool, ToolPixi, DailyWallPix, and MythPost — and every new page on each of them goes through the same small daily routine: one Search Console session, one fixed time of day, about ten indexing requests, and a written queue so nothing is asked twice. There is no trick in this checklist. The trick is doing the small thing every day instead of the big thing once.

Before you ask Google anything

  • Confirm the page returns a real 200 on the custom domain — not on a staging address, not on a redirect.
  • Check the canonical tag points at the real URL of the page itself, not at the homepage.
  • Make sure the page is worth indexing: real content a stranger would read, not a stub or a duplicate.
  • Confirm the sitemap lists the page and that the sitemap itself loads at its real address.
  • Check that at least one other page on your site links to it — a page nothing links to is a page nobody, including Google, can find.

The one-door rule

  • Use one Search Console door only. Do not keep signing in fresh, signing out, or opening new sessions — every fresh sign-in is a chance to get locked out.
  • Keep one queue file: every new URL in the order it was published, oldest first. The queue is the plan.
  • Run indexing once a day, at the same fixed time. Hamza's run is every evening.
  • Never submit a URL that is already in the queue. Asking twice does not make Google faster.

The daily run — about ten a day

  • Open the queue and take the next ten URLs — about ten requests a day is the working limit, so spend them on the oldest pages first.
  • For each URL: inspect it in Search Console, then request indexing.
  • Write down what went in and the date, and remove it from the queue. The log is what stops duplicates.
  • Stop when the day's requests are used. Extra requests past the limit do not help and only waste the next day's run.
  • If the session is dead — signed out, verification asked again — stop the run. Do not force sign-ins from a script; wait for a proper sign-in.

While you wait

  • Indexing takes days. A page requested today is not a page found tomorrow — keep publishing while you wait.
  • Do not re-request a URL that is already waiting. Patience is part of the system.
  • Check coverage once a week: real errors — 404s, redirect chains, blocked pages — get fixed before anything is re-requested.
  • If a whole batch of pages is not appearing, look at the pages first: thin content and duplicate titles are a content problem, not an indexing problem.
  • Count only what Search Console actually shows. Small honest numbers tell you where to work next.
