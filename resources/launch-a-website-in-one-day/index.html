Launch a Website in One Day — Checklist

The one-day launch system behind MythPost: the slow work done in advance, the launch-day sequence, and the first week after — 42 articles live the same evening.

On 10 November 2026, Hamza launched MythPost, a fourth website, in a single day: 42 complete articles, 57 pages in total, 161 files deployed to Cloudflare Pages, and 3,654 internal links checked with none broken. The site went live the same evening. One day sounds fast — and it was, but only because the slow work was already done. This checklist is that launch in one page: what to prepare before the day, what to do on it, and what the first week after looks like.

Before launch day — the slow work

  • Build the machine first: the publishing system, the page templates, the image rules, and the policy pages — all finished before launch day, not during it.
  • Decide the deployment route in advance and test it once, so launch day holds no surprises.
  • Write the content before the day starts. Launch day is for shipping, not for writing from zero.
  • Buy the domain early and keep the nameserver change ready to make the same day.
  • Decide what launch-day success looks like in one sentence — a live site with real content, not a perfect one.

Launch day — build, check, deploy

  • Build the full site and count what you shipped: pages, articles, files.
  • Check every internal link before deploying — Hamza's launch had 3,654 links checked and none broken.
  • Deploy to Cloudflare Pages and open the pages.dev address like a stranger: homepage, a category page, several inner pages.
  • Confirm the deployed file count matches your build count. If it does not, stop and find the gap.
  • Change the nameservers the same day, so the custom domain activates while the launch is still fresh.

After launch — the first week

  • Submit the sitemap to Search Console as soon as the custom domain is live, and request indexing for your most important pages.
  • Publish five to ten original articles a day while the topic is fresh — the launch is a start, not a finish.
  • Keep the daily posting cadence going on your social pages; distribution is usually the bottleneck, not the work.
  • Work the click gap: pages already ranking on page two and three need better titles and better answers, not more pages.
  • Count only numbers you verified, and write them down. Small honest numbers tell you where to work next.

When distribution breaks

  • Expect the break to be in distribution, not in the content: a blocked domain, a signed-out account, a filtered comment.
  • Fix the block first, then keep publishing — do not stop the cadence while one channel is down.
  • If a format fails twice, drop it. Not every experiment deserves a third try.
  • Write the failures down in the same place as the wins, so the next launch learns from both.
