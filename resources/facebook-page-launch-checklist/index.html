Facebook Page Launch Checklist

The launch and daily system behind Hamza's Facebook pages — setup, first posts, fixed slots, and the caption rule his pages never break.

Hamza's Facebook pages publish every day for a mostly USA audience, and they run on a system, not on inspiration. This checklist is that system in one page: how a page is set up, what the first posts must do, and the daily and weekly habits that keep it growing. It costs nothing but consistency — which is exactly the point.

Before you launch

  • Pick one clear page name and handle — one promise a stranger understands in three seconds.
  • Write the one-line promise: who the page is for and what it posts, every day.
  • Use a profile image and cover that still read clearly at thumbnail size.
  • Complete the About section: bio, contact route, and your website link.
  • Decide your page's topics and voice before post one — and never mix another page's voice into it.

The first posts

  • Prepare your first nine posts before you announce the page to anyone.
  • Pin your strongest post — the one that says what the page stands for.
  • Every post must pass the accuracy check: no errors in the quote, the fact, or the caption. Accuracy first, always.
  • Captions stay short — two or three words and one emoji, in your page's voice. Zero hashtags: captions carry reach, hashtags do not.
  • Design every post for people who do not follow you yet — a strong first three seconds and a reason to share.

The daily system

  • Post at the same fixed times every day; a rhythm your audience can set a clock by.
  • Never repeat a quote or a topic — keep a registry of everything you have used.
  • Read every post once more before it goes out, at full size, the way a stranger will see it.
  • Reply to early comments in the first hour — early conversation is part of the post.
  • Stagger pages 20–30 minutes apart if you run more than one, so you can answer comments on each.

The weekly review

  • Open Insights once a week: reach, new followers, and your top three posts.
  • Double down on the topics that travelled; retire formats that flatline twice in a row.
  • Check the whole week's board, not just one post — a missed slot is a broken promise.
  • Write down one thing to improve next week. One — then actually do it.
